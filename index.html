Case Studies

Three representative undertakings, set out in the order of problem, approach, and result, spanning the full stack from data architecture to the interface.

A

Enterprise BI Design System

Problem

Reporting tools across an enterprise BI platform each looked and behaved differently - forcing users to relearn every dashboard, driving high cognitive load, and pushing people to export to spreadsheets instead of trusting the tools.

Approach

Authored a company-first Power BI UX/UI standards guide plus a copy-paste-ready (Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V) component library that supplements the enterprise UX community of excellence and global design system. Defined a 12-column × 6-row anchoring grid (91px columns, 78px rows, 18px margins), a Segoe UI type scale, WCAG AA/AAA color pairings with documented contrast ratios, Google Material iconography (18/24px), and reusable primary/secondary headers, footers, filter panels, cards, tables, and graphs - even a governed Copilot narrative-summary pattern. Led a cross-team working group to co-create and deploy it.

Outcome

Transformed enterprise reporting with a unified design language.

1

Enterprise Data Publishing Pipeline

2

Metadata Distillation Pipeline

The lakehouse held thousands of tables and years of analysts' notebooks - far too much for any human, or any AI context window, to reason over. I engineered a pipeline that turns that noise into the compact "brain" an AI assistant can actually trust: deduplicating notebooks, ranking tables by real usage, mining join relationships, and extracting a living domain glossary.

~600 MB → ~230 KB of pure signal - a ~2,600× reduction. Mapped ~5,900 tables, ~4,400 join keys, and ~350 real query patterns; removed 860+ duplicates. This is the grounding layer that stops the assistant from ever hallucinating a table name.
3

Cloud Storage Architecture

Re-architected attachment handling to offload large binary files into cloud object storage, serving them as lightweight links inside the lakehouse and BI reports.

Reduced database bloat and enabled clean in-report file downloads.

Concept to working prototype in under 1 week. No hallucinated schemas.

Process & Approach
How I Work

Presented not as an inventory of tools but as the principles by which these products are conceived and built, from the first question to the finished result.

The request is seldom the requirement

A request for a chart is frequently, in substance, a request for a decision. Before a table is modeled or a screen is drawn, the underlying question is established: who must act, upon what, and by what measure the action will be judged to have succeeded.

The work is carried end to end

The data is architected, the pipeline and the intelligence above it engineered, and the interface designed, by a single hand. Where responsibility passes intact from the model to the screen, the trust built into the foundation is preserved in what the user is finally shown.

Nothing is asserted that cannot be traced

Whether the source is a governed dataset or a distilled context supplied to an AI system, every answer is made to rest upon something verifiable. Tables are not invented and figures are not imagined; where certainty is absent, the absence is stated plainly.

Judgment is deferred to evidence

A working prototype is reached in days rather than quarters, and then placed before those who will use it. Satisfaction, task time, and adoption are measured, so that the decisions which follow rest upon observation rather than upon assertion.

A result is made to compound

A single success is of limited consequence; a reusable standard is not. What proves effective is set down as component libraries, scorecards, and instruction, so that the benefit extends beyond the particular report to the organization that depends upon it.

About Me
Mitchell Ray
Data & AI Product Builder · Engineering + UX
Greater Cincinnati, OH

For more than seven years I have worked on data and AI products at a Fortune 50 consumer goods company, most recently in a research specialist capacity to which I was promoted. The work does not sit within a single discipline. In the course of a given project I architect the data pipelines and models, build the AI and retrieval systems that operate upon them, and design the interface through which the result is at last used, proceeding from the raw source data to the point at which a person comes to depend upon it.

I am as much at home within a medallion lakehouse, designing dimensional models, publishing governed data, and engineering retrieval and semantic layers, as I am conducting usability testing and establishing design systems. This breadth is deliberate. It permits a data and AI product to be carried from its architecture through to the experience of its users by a single hand, and its results to be demonstrated in measured terms.

Over this period I have guided the manner in which a large research and development organization designs, interprets, and scales its data. That work has included the authoring of enterprise interface standards and reusable component libraries, the redesign of high-traffic reporting products serving tens of thousands of users, and the design of a conversational data-discovery assistant that reduced a search of several steps to a matter of seconds.

Throughout, user research and usability testing are held against a standard of measurable value, whether in speed to insight, in adoption, or in the reduction of cognitive load. I hold a Bachelor of Science in Environmental Science with a concentration in Data Analytics, conferred Summa Cum Laude, and I am a certified Professional Scrum Product Owner (PSPO I).

Flagship training

A hands-on programme of six modules that carries a team from a business problem through to a finished, shareable report, in the sequence of business problem, data model, analysis, visualization, and distribution. No background in programming or statistics is assumed; a person who has arranged and linked data within a spreadsheet already possesses the essential ideas. Each module observes the same order, concept, practice, application, and reflection, and is directed toward a demonstrable result rather than a lecture, so that those attending leave able to resolve a problem of their own.

1

Data Models at Scale

Relational databases, star schemas, fact vs. dimension tables, and primary/foreign keys - taught with an unplugged "human data model" exercise that makes relationships click before anyone touches a tool.

2

Reporting with a BI Tool

Power BI fundamentals: model, table, and report views; building your first table and slicers; fixing aggregations; and why a modeled BI tool removes the "curiosity tax" a spreadsheet charges.

3

Connecting, Cleaning & Transforming

Where data should live, connecting to new sources, and cleaning responsibly - nulls vs. real blanks, data types, repeatable transformation steps, and the ethics of cleaning without misleading.

4

Accessing Enterprise Data with SQL

A little SQL unlocks a lot of data: queries, catalogs, and workspaces on a cloud data platform - plus using a GenAI assistant to draft and explain queries, always reviewed before you trust the output.

5

Visualization & Communication

Message, audience, and "is a chart even worth it?" Tables vs. visuals, choosing the right chart, decluttering, and the 10-second rule - a beautiful chart answering the wrong question is worse than a plain table answering the right one.

6

Verifying, Validating & Sharing

"How would I know if this were wrong?" Sanity checks, SME review, formal validation for high-stakes reports, and publishing responsibly so the right report reaches the right people.
